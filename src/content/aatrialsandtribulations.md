This game was a pleasant surprise after the last installment, providing impressive and unique mysteries that entrench the player in Ace Attorney's world and characters. For once, the first case, Turnabout Memories, is deeply rooted in the story of the game as you experience Mia Fey's second trial as a defense attorney. Yet this time, she is defending a young Phoenix Wright. Starting the game this way immediately roped me in, building a heavy intrigue with the both return of known characters and an introduction to new ones. The game lacks slightly in the second case, The Stolen Turnabout, and even more in the third case, Recipe for Turnabout, but neither are outright bad. The former being far more enjoyable of the two chapters. Yet both cases are necessary for sections of the final chapters to land as well as they eventually do. The fourth chapter, Turnabout Beginnings, is a direct call back to the first and was highly anticipated for me. The case gives you so much more information and lore about Mia Fey and the new prosecutor for the game, Godot. While also building on a deeply intriguing character from the first case. But the true star of this game is the fifth and final case, Bridge to the Turnabout, one of the few cases in the trilogy that rocked me to my core and had me completely and utterly invested from start to finish. The case wraps up the game greatly, but it doesn't stop there as it acts as a culmination of the entire original Ace Attorney trilogy. This case has it all, it brings back beloved characters, answers the burning mysteries that were previously planted, calls back to previous cases in both this game and previous games, and becomes quite the tear jerker when you reach the final answers.

The main characters are written so well and have such a deep connection to one another, but also to the player by this point, with realizations of just how deeply these characters are bonded or have changed over their arcs. The music was fantastic, every track fits so perfectly in each moment. I could listen to Godot's theme for hours on end, truthfully sometimes I sat and listened to it while he was on screen! Overall, this game is the perfect end to the original Ace Attorney trilogy and built excitement for me to see where things go in the remainder of the series. Alas, we must address the elephant in the room as such an amazing game doesn't earn a ten. Honestly, I so desperately want to give this game a full ten but my dislike for the third episode just can't allow me to let it slide. The third case contained so many characters that I disliked, either by personality or design, regardless of how interesting the trial may have been.
