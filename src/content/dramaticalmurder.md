**Content Warning: May contain content that is triggering to some, including sexual harassment and assault and gore. This review also contains spoilers for the featured game.**

_Dramatical Murder_ is a boys’ love visual novel developed by Nitro+CHiRaL and published internationally by JAST USA. It follows Aoba Seragaki, an employee at a local second hand shop in Midorijima, a fictional island off the coast of Japan. Living in a cyberpunk world full of electronic companions, cyber games, and violent turf wars, Aoba is quickly wrapped up in more than he could ever imagine.

# Common Route

_Dramatical Murder_ begins with an introduction to the loveable main character, Aoba Seragaki, an employee at the local junk shop who has an irresistible voice, chronic migraines, and hair that can feel. Seriously, his hair has nerves. You are quickly introduced to the various aspects of Midorijima including its gang wars, technological companions called Allmates, and the big Toue corporation lingering over everything. The common route has a bit of a slow start, taking its time to explain the ins and outs of the Old Residential District where Aoba lives and the various impacts of Toue’s acquisition of the island. At the time, I felt that the common route was a little boring and I constantly felt like I was waiting for more…but after finishing the game I genuinely believe the common route is the perfect way to settle into the world, set stakes, and meet the characters.

One of the first things the player is introduced to is the concept of Ribsteez and Rhyme. The former is the name of a “game” set in the physical realm that is centered around players creating gangs, claiming territory, and fighting one another. On the other hand, Rhyme is a similar experience except all fighting is done in a virtual world with Allmates, or AI companions. I really enjoyed the natural and easy conflicts the existence of Rib and Rhyme gangs creates. Not only does it create the precedent that the area Aoba calls home may be dangerous, it also sets the stage for future conflicts between characters. Over the course of the common route, characters are introduced and stakes are slowly built until it all comes collapsing down with a realization that Toue is attempting to pursue some form of mass mind control.

Towards the end of the common route, Aoba’s grandmother is kidnapped and a close friend of his, Mizuki, goes missing at the hands of a mysterious gang called Morphine. While trying to save his grandmother, Aoba discovers an ability hidden deep within him called Scrap. Scrap allows him to enter the minds of others and speak with them, but what he does in Scrap can have drastically different outcomes on the individual. It is here that Aoba breaks Mizuki’s mind, altering the state of it and nearly killing him. This is truly what I felt to be the turning point of the game. Watching Aoba accidentally break Mizuki’s mind is haunting and uproots a lot of the calmness from early in the game. We’ve known the world Aoba lives in is dangerous and cruel, but we’ve never seen Aoba become dangerous too. This is the first time we learn that Aoba truly is harboring a force to be reckoned with and to the player it’s scary!

It’s at this point that Aoba is set on facing Toue in Platinum Jail, the amusement facility at the heart of Midorijima. Here is where the routes split off between characters, with a different character joining Aoba in his pursuit.

Overall, the common route serves as a great way to introduce the world to the player and builds on the romanceable characters, creating a lot of intrigue in what they all have to offer. While it may be slow to start and spend a lot of time explaining concepts, without it the later half of the game would suffer greatly. Every stake and mystery is set within the common route, particularly in a way that almost goes unnoticed to the player until they start to become more relevant in the other routes.

# Koujaku’s Route

Koujaku is Aoba’s childhood friend, though they briefly lost touch, they’ve had a strong friendship since Koujaku’s return to Midorijima. Koujaku is a flirtatious traveling hairdresser and extremely popular with the ladies, often having an entourage of women following him. He leads the Rib group named Benishigure, a smaller gang with a larger heart than most.

Koujaku’s route felt much more rooted in him as a character, as opposed to the world or threats. Over the course of the route, we slowly learn more and more about the horrors Koujaku faced during his time on the mainland. This includes learning that Koujaku is also a victim of Toue, being used at a young age to test a method of mind control through tattoos. This makes Koujaku a slave to his tattoo artist, Ryuho, who they encounter in Platinum Jail. I loved this reveal so much, the obvious pain Koujaku went through during his time on the mainland is abundantly clear and now he is forever haunted by tattoos to remind him of it. Typically a cool minded individual, his tattoos make him almost a ravenous beast if he gets too angry or stressed which is a contrast that truly tickles the brain. The concept of tattoos as a form of mind control feels very unique here and it isn’t as potent as full mind control, rather as if it opens a weakness in Koujaku’s mind to exploit which is a much more interesting way to control someone despite being less effective.

Aoba’s relationship with Koujaku is also a very prominent part of the route, as will be a recurring theme given the nature of the game. I typically do not find the childhood friends to lovers trope all that interesting with a lot of examples of it falling flat or feeling half baked, but I think Koujaku and Aoba have one of the best written relationships in this game. The common route does an amazing job of setting up an established friendship between the two that makes them feel close and shows genuine care for one another. Then the moment you step into Platinum Jail you are hit with the most angstiest angst that has ever been. Misunderstandings, fights, mistakes, hurt, these two have it all and boy is it delicious and it’s all centered around Koujaku just wanting to keep Aoba safe from Ryuho.

But at the same time there is the perfect amount of confusion as Aoba wonders why he cares so much, creating this slow switch from long time friends to a crush where the player cannot perfectly pin when the shift happens. It’s perfectly done with so much questioning and yearning and the result is really profound once the two can finally learn to embrace one another and accept their feelings. It feels sudden at times, but it's rewarding to see the two finally reach a point of bliss where they can live their mundane lives together. Seeing the good ending where Koujaku is finally able to cut Aoba’s hair was genuinely all I had ever wanted by this point, the two are so at peace and so in love!

Each route does have a bad ending. Koujaku’s is a perfect match to his character and his relationship with Aoba. Aoba has lost control to the powers nesting with himself and Koujaku has let his tattoos consume him, creating a dynamic that puts Aoba in control and Koujaku completely dependent on him. Aoba allowing an almost beast-like Koujaku to drink his blood is the perfect bad ending to take for these two characters, leaning hard on Aoba’s desire to be there for Koujaku and Koujaku’s beastly form when he begins to lose control.

Overall this route is a stellar introduction to _Dramatical Murder_. It doesn’t push the envelope too much but sets a certain tone for a lot of the other routes. There are some dicey moments but it manages to avoid anything too outwardly uncomfortable, at least in comparison to later routes. It doesn’t do a lot of world building besides introducing the player to Platinum Jail in a very casual way, as if Aoba is genuinely a citizen there. But it is typically the route players do first, in which it succeeds in being so approachable and building the world in a way that is not overwhelming. It essentially allows the later routes to assume the idea that the player is likely to have familiarity with Platinum Jail and focus on more important things. If childhood friends to lovers, angst, and yearning are your thing, then Koujaku’s route is perfect!

# Noiz's Route

Noiz is a quiet yet aloof Rhyme player and hacker that Aoba encounters. He acts as an information broker for Rhyme, being able to determine when Rhyme matches will be started and where. Noiz is clearly interested in getting Aoba to play Rhyme, going as far as pulling him into matches without rules (ganking) to attempt to achieve his goal. He also runs a Rhyme gang, Ruff Rabbits, though it is implied they are primarily an information-broking group. With his stand-offish personality, many piercings, and punk style, it likely isn’t a surprise that Noiz was my most anticipated route.

Much like the route before this, Noiz’s route is very much rooted in his character more than any extreme world building. He begins as somewhat of a rival or adversary to Aoba, always trying to fight him in Rhyme and egg him on. As a big fan of enemies to lovers, this was like crack to me and oh boy did the writers lean into this. Aoba’s constant desire to care for anyone around him next to Noiz’s inability to care for anyone, even himself, is the most angsty and dramatic pairing ever. Consistently we see Aoba put himself in danger to protect Noiz followed by Noiz being completely confused by this action and getting angry at Aoba. Or Aoba goes out of his way to take care of Noiz while he is injured, and once again Noiz reacts with confusion and anger. While at times this feels a little repetitive it isn’t a drag by any means. Truthfully, Aoba and Noiz have a ton of chemistry and you can tell the pull that they have on one another. Even their first intimacy scene is pretty much these two egging one another on until they find themselves in a precarious situation together.

As the two continue to explore Platinum Jail and pursue Toue at the central tower, Oval Tower, it becomes clear that something is different about Noiz. During a very harmful encounter with security in the Oval Tower, Aoba ends up brutally wounded after taking a hit for Noiz and the two begin arguing once more about why Aoba would do such a thing. The scene that follows is tense, yet heartwarming, as Aoba discovers that Noiz can’t feel most sensations, including pain. All Aoba wants to do is keep the person he cares for safe, but now Noiz feels the same way. The two are in a loop of just trying to protect one another, each at their own cost and regardless of if they can feel pain or not.

The way Noiz’s disability is handled up to this point is very well integrated into his character. Can you imagine how someone might turn out if they are incapable of feeling pain, understanding pain, and how it feels? You’d be cold, apathetic, and a loner because you have no way of truly understanding another human. But despite this, Aoba is able to show Noiz what it is like to care for someone, to understand pain even if he can’t feel it, and accept what it might be like to be with another person. He holds Noiz close, doing things he finds personally comforting to show Noiz that not everything is against him. It’s beautiful and one of the most raw scenes in the game, at least for me.

Following this moment, Noiz leaves Aoba to find a way upstairs but an unsettling amount of time passes. Worried, Aoba eventually begins to search on his own and reaches the top of Oval Tower where he finds Noiz… under Toue’s mind control. Aoba is forced to use Scrap on Noiz, which is when we learn that abuse from his parents caused his inability to feel. Noiz’s Scrap segment is very raw, showing how he feels in comparison to other people, an outcast, a loner, a parasite. It uses elements of code rot to show just how Noiz can feel around others, as if he is just a glitched out black void compared to them. This is a phenomenon completely unique to Noiz’s Scrap segment and it solidifies his deep connection to technology.

In the good ending, Aoba is able to restore Noiz’s ability to feel and this realization really breathes a second life into Noiz. To no surprise, he is quick to throw himself in harm's way to protect Aoba during their escape from the collapsing tower. This pain must be excruciating for Noiz, yet he still puts himself in danger to keep Aoba safe from falling rubble and gets some pretty serious injuries. Despite a broken arm and cuts all over, he isn’t overwhelmed by the pain but rather in awe at his ability to feel it. I love this so much because it is very in character. If you search even a little deeper into who Noiz is, it is abundantly clear that Noiz truly cares for Aoba more than he lets on. It is more than just a physical attraction, he wants to keep him safe. The Noiz who has just discovered pain would not shy away from how excruciating it may be, as long as Aoba is safe. The two escape and the epilogue reveals that Noiz returned home to Germany to set things right with his family, sporting a very piercing-less look sadly. I wasn’t a huge fan of this, considering his family is partially to blame for his trauma but I want to trust that this will have better implications in the game’s sequel.

As for the bad ending, it takes place in Noiz's head while Aoba uses Scrap when “a real world” is chosen. A real world to Noiz is one where he can feel, yet in Scrap it manifests as a brutal, agonizing, neverending pain for him and Aoba together. While gory and sad, it’s the perfect bad ending for Noiz as it shows the extremity of his desire to be able to touch and feel pain. The two writhe in pain but can’t bring themselves to separate, out of pure desire to be together.

Overall, I think Noiz is a very compelling character tucked into this game. There is more to him than what meets the eye and his cold exterior is eventually broken down in a way that feels very rewarding by the end of the game. There are so many amazing details that go into his character that center around his inability to feel pain, such as playing Rhyme so he can feel the mental pain, or having a ton of piercings because they were never painful to get. He seems like such a surface level character, yet hides so much of his true character within his route. The escape from Oval Tower makes for a very rich ending, even if the epilogue falls flat. What Noiz’s route sacrifices in world building, it makes up for with such a unique character. Without a doubt this is my favorite route, I genuinely think Noiz and Aoba are the perfect pair out of all the options here.

# Mink's Route

Mink is the intimidating, tall, muscular leader of Scratch, a gang made of escaped prison inmates. He is a mysterious character, never revealing his true intentions to anyone but rather using them as pawns to achieve his goals. He comes from a Native American background and has an intense love for his tribe.

It’s time to get into what is probably the most gut-wrenching and downright hard to read route in this game. I can only guess that Mink was created to fulfill the weird requirement of at least one edgy and violent route that Nitro+CHiRaL games typically feature. Even his initial introduction to Aoba is brutal and terrifying as Mink orders his gang to sexually assault Aoba as a way to force him to use his voice and lose control.

I’ll likely talk about the plot of this route the least, as it doesn’t feel genuinely engaging and contains a lot of content that is hard to discuss. We learn that Mink is trying to get revenge on Toue. Mink’s village was infamous for having an herbal recipe that could control a person’s scent, but Toue thought this might be another potential method for mind control. Refusal to hand over the recipe led to the massacre of Mink’s entire village and years of him being subjected to experiments. This motivation is what leads Mink into leveraging Aoba into a deal, if Mink helps save Aoba’s grandmother then Aoba has to help Mink in return, eventually bringing them to Platinum Jail and the start of their route.

Over the course of the route it becomes clear that Mink is obsessed with pulling out the true Aoba, otherwise known as Sly Blue. But the way Mink approaches this is by repeatedly beating and sexually assaulting Aoba. I’m so serious, the entire route is Aoba constantly being abused by Mink (sometimes even while under the influence) just so Mink can attempt to access Aoba’s ability to use Scrap. Every single one of these encounters with one another is really hard to sit through and read. I played nearly the entire route with a disgusted and appalled look on my face. There are brief moments and acts of kindness from Mink that somewhat show that he cares or is remorseful but absolutely none of them are at a large enough scale to forgive literally anything else.

More notably, this is the first route where we actually get to see Toue in the flesh since Mink’s backstory directly involves him. I was really excited about this as until now, Toue has only haunted the narrative and is inadvertently defeated due to the collapse of Oval Tower. This time the characters are actually able to face off against Toue and it makes for a much more rewarding reveal and a great peek into how crazy this man really is.

After Aoba uses Scrap on Mink to save him from Toue’s mind control music, Aoba makes his escape and leaves Mink behind to face Toue one on one. Mink had always planned to die after
getting his revenge, this leaves Aoba in a depressive state after returning to his regular life. But he sets out to find Mink, feeling that he really can’t be dead. After a long and arduous journey, the two are eventually reunited at the remains of Mink’s village. I felt very conflicted by this narrative choice, here is Aoba relentlessly chasing after this man who abused him over and over again. Furthermore, it’s very clear that the two have proceeded to build a life together as well. It was jarring, feeling almost out of nowhere, as I genuinely wasn’t sure that Aoba truly liked Mink.

This route is met with a lot of hate in this fandom and is a constant topic for arguments. There is an obviously very good reason for that. Walking away from it, I was very disgusted by Mink’s actions and the way he treated Aoba. I felt as though Aoba just had this desire to return to Mink due to a trauma bond created between the two. While I do not like Mink, or excuse his actions, I’m able to recognize that he is an impressively complex character. There is more to him and his actions than my initial thoughts and he is the definition of cyclical abuse. He isn’t a genuinely evil person, even if some of his actions are incredibly violent and completely misplaced. Once Mink is able to pull out the “real Aoba”, he is scared. He knows that he was wrong, that this powerful and aggressive alter-ego is not the real Aoba. His actions do adjust slightly after that scene, showing small notions of affection to Aoba and praying while he sleeps. I can absolutely recognize that Mink is an insanely hard character to understand, while still fundamentally disliking him for his actions. Truthfully, I am excited to see the continuation of his endings in the sequel because I am curious what a version of Mink that is not driven by vengeance looks like. How will he treat Aoba? What is the real Mink like?

Oh, and the bad ending? Yeah Mink decapitates Aoba while they are inside his mind… Still feels like we might be perpetuating some abuse here.

# Clear's Route

Clear is an odd yet polite character with no clear origin upon introduction, just a boy in a gas mask and a white trench coat. He suddenly appears and insists on calling Aoba “master”. Clear is definitely the least fleshed-out character based on the common route alone, which makes for a very compelling story later on.

In a complete and total contrast to the previous route I played, Clear’s route is easily the tamest and most digestible of any of the characters. Right off the bat, Clear jumps into just trying to serve Aoba by making food and taking Aoba to his bed after he falls asleep on the couch. It becomes obvious that this is simply Clear’s nature.

A large majority of this route focuses on Aoba really coming to understand Clear and learn more about him, starting with requesting to see his face. Aoba asks a few times about the topic, but Clear seems to try to brush it off or respond with gags. It isn’t till push comes to shove that Clear actually takes his mask off. During an encounter with a gang that goes sideways, Clear has acid dumped on him that, by all means, should be deadly. Yet he seems to have no reaction to it, leading Aoba to worry once the two have removed themselves from the situation. He begs Clear to take off his mask and wash his face in a nearby fountain and after some resistance, Clear obliges. Aoba’s reaction to seeing Clear’s face is nothing short of pure romanticism, that boy is in love! He immediately reassures the white-haired boy that he is handsome and that he shouldn’t be so afraid of showing his face. We learn that Clear wears the mask because of his grandfather, who told him to never show his face. I love this entire scene so much because it feels like one of the few times you can genuinely see Aoba fall in love. None of the other characters have a scene that distinctly shows a shift in feelings, which I think is intentional. But with Clear it feels like Aoba was in love at first sight. Clear also gets a lot of reassurance and comfort after Aoba affirms that Clear's face is "just like his", having eyes, a nose, and a mouth. It really deconstructs Clear's negative feelings about how he might look since there is some built up trauma from years of hiding behind a mask.

By this point, there have been a few events that lead Aoba to believe that Clear may not be human, namely his lack of injury from the acid and his lack of understanding of social interactions. This was something I had suspected since Mink’s route as I noticed Clear’s hair bears a striking resemblance to Toue’s guards and was practically confirmed for me when I realized how much Clear sings. This idea isn’t confirmed until Clear and Aoba have a run-in with two Platinum Jail guards that look exactly like Clear. Clear is actually an android created by Toue, both to function as a guard and to sing mind-controlling music. This entire encounter is tense as Clear attacks the other guards, resulting in some serious damage to himself. But the two also share a very sweet moment together where Clear confesses his feelings for Aoba, probably the earliest confession of any route. The moment is tender and authentic, even though we have just learned that Clear is artificial. I love Aoba’s total confusion in this scene too, it’s so obvious that he feels the same way but isn’t ready to admit that. Despite that, he is so gentle with Clear and never makes him feel outright ashamed for his feelings.

Not long after, the two pursue Toue into the Oval Tower for a final showdown. Clear had to bypass a component to attack the guards earlier, which Toue reveals the true consequence of this action. This has started the slow disintegration of Clear’s body. We begin to see his skin flake away and his mechanical features beneath are exposed. This finality he is faced with forces Clear to take an unthinkable action, driving a knife into his neck to sever the component that makes him unable to fight any other android created by Toue. Aoba is able to use Scrap here, but not using it is what results in the good ending. This entire scene turns our loveable Clear into a total badass who would do anything to keep Aoba safe.

Giving Clear his own autonomy allows him to successfully fight off Toue and his guards, with music of course! The two escape and return to their place of stay but the damage to Clear is unstoppable. In his final moments the two share an intimate moment together, per Clear’s dying wish. While initially weirded out by the idea of being intimate with someone who is actively dying, it is a lot less weird with a character who is a robot. The moment feels very final, but as the player you are aware that it doesn’t necessarily have to be this way considering Aoba’s knowledge of performing mechanical repairs. Unsurprisingly, that is exactly what happens. Aoba brings Clear’s body home and begins to do small repairs to him before sending him to a professional. Though the chances seemed unlikely, Clear is eventually restored and the two reunite. This ending makes for easily the most emotional of the four, Aoba’s disbelief and Clear’s calmness is perfect. Not to mention, I love the idea that so many of these models were made, but Aoba loves Clear specifically, as if he is his own person. I’m deeply excited to see these two expanded upon in the sequel.

Yet, if Aoba were to cause Clear to hesitate by using Scrap, Clear would be unable to successfully defeat Toue. This results in Clear being essentially factory reset and reprogrammed by Toue. Clear is then put in a position where he is the primary “caretaker” of Aoba, who is now Toue’s prisoner. For the character with the sweetest route, this bad ending is down right horrific. It’s cold and sterile like a hospital, breathing pure dread into your heart as you see the state Aoba is in because of Toue’s abuse through the control Clear.

Overall, I did not find it surprising that Clear’s route is the most popular amongst fans. He is easily the most palatable character out of everyone. There aren’t any instances of questionable moral actions, just two boys falling in love while very much in danger. Playing this after Mink's route was like a breath of fresh air and reminded me that I actually don’t hate this game! Clear and Aoba really make a lot of sense together, feeling like opposites yet similar enough to click in just the right ways. But at the same time, the route and story here felt underwhelming. Clear feels like much more of a surface level character in comparison to characters like Mink or Noiz. The entire premise is also a lot less unique, a defective android who craves humanity and was built by the villian of the entire game. I enjoyed Clear's route, but it stands out the least when stood next the three other romanceable options.

# True Route

Clearing all four good endings unlocks the true route, also referred to as Ren’s route. Ren is Aoba’s Allmate, a fluffy robotic dog that accompanies Aoba everywhere. Yes, the dog. Trust me, I had the same reaction.

You are required to replay the entire game here, with a few new options popping up that favor Ren but don’t result in any grand changes. The route eventually kicks off with the two entering Platinum Jail, of course. It isn’t long after their arrival that Ren begins acting strangely and distancing himself from Aoba. This was a very good choice, creating necessary drama to heighten interest. Otherwise I think the story would’ve started to stall due to the limited interactions the two can have.

The day of Toue’s celebration begins to creep closer, the day he plans to use mind control to take hold of all of Midorijima. With just days remaining, Toue introduces the world to Sei, a young sickly boy who is able to control minds with just his eyes. This was the point where I started to get excited, it felt extremely clear that this route would have a lot more content in regards to the overall story that the previous routes have built. As if his mind control could work on me, I was entranced by Sei and so excited to finally get some story pay off.

The game absolutely delivers by wrapping up every question you’ve ever had as the plot continues. Ren and Aoba rush into the Oval Tower the following day, but Ren ends up damaged beyond repair. Attempting to find a place to rest, Aoba wanders into a room with test tubes and human-like test subjects. Disgusted, Aoba attempts to leave but is stopped by an entire horde of people who are headed by two men, Virus and Trip. Two side characters within the common route who don’t serve a major purpose besides keeping an eye on Aoba. These two drop a ton of lore: Aoba is a lab baby, Sei is his twin brother, these two are the leaders of the dangerous gang named Morphine, and they wiped Aoba’s memories as a teenager. I was SAT! I had been so excited to learn more about Morphine as they haunt so much of the common route and hadn’t even thought that Virus and Trip may be a part of that. These two were already hot and mysterious, but this just made them way more intriguing. Besides just that, we get so much information about Aoba’s history that changes everything we thought we knew. This moment is the payoff of hours of this game setting up mystery after mystery for its player to ponder.

All of this eventually takes us to a final Rhyme showdown with Toue with so much aura that it made me wish the game were an RPG. What follows Toue’s defeat is also a heartfelt moment between Sei and Aoba. These two have never even known one another, yet you can tell they sympathize deeply. Despite their different upbringings, the two can relate so much to one another thanks to the nature of their birth and Toue’s abuse. Even if Sei’s appearance is short, he became a deeply treasured character of mine. By all means he should’ve been a threat, seeking vengeance on all who have hurt him up til this point. Yet his only wish is for a peaceful death so that his pain might finally end. I fear it touches on a soft spot I have for characters who know their fates and take it at face value. I also totally think Sei has some genderqueer coding that is super subtle but absolutely there if you have the eyes for it. Maybe I’m biased but I love any character that explores gender in an nontraditional way, even if they are canonically cisgender.

The part that really irks me about this route is everything to do with Ren. There is just a sudden reveal that Ren is a part of Aoba’s consciousness, born to protect him, who eventually transferred himself to an Allmate to better perform his job. And the reason Ren’s been acting so weird? All due to a common bug in Allmates that's only mentioned this one time. And the reason Ren’s Allmate vessel no longer works? He bit Aoba to transfer himself back into Aoba’s consciousness. If this is bizarre to you, then imagine my reaction because this is about the only explanation you ever get about the topic. To save Ren, Aoba uses Scrap on himself to enter his own mind and remind Ren that he truly cares for him.

The good ending results in Ren overcoming this bug affecting him and leading the two to share an intimate moment within Aoba’s mind. Their relationship is somewhat “self-cest” and somewhat not. But no matter how hard I tried to not think too hard about it, Aoba mentions how weird it is that the two are able to kiss and embrace one another. He even goes as far as blatantly commenting on the fact that he's essentially kissing himself. But somehow we aren’t even at the weirdest part. The epilogue reveals that Sei gave up his body so that Ren could have a physical form…so now we are just getting as close to incest as we possibly can without actually doing it. Morally it feels wrong but considering Sei and Aoba were lab grown, it helps make the entire thing a smidge less weird. This wasn’t really unexpected for a game like this, but it is easily the weirdest part of the entire game.

The true ending did a lot of things right, it answers any and all remaining questions the player has up till this point. Some of these are genuinely mind blowing reveals, such as Sei being the sender of the video game messages Aoba receives that predict future events in the common route. I really relished in the major story beats, but it feels like the romance with Ren really drags a lot of this down. More often than not I found their relationship odd and seemingly random, detracting a substantial amount from the story. There is already a lot going on that needs careful explanation, I don’t think adding the existence of Ren being a part of Aoba that got transferred into an Allmate was really necessary. It bogs down the story with something that almost makes no sense. I would’ve preferred a true ending that didn’t attach itself to a romanceable partner.

# Closing Thoughts

_Dramatical Murder_ is a thoroughly unique experience, something you cannot begin to prepare yourself for. It is far from a perfect game or story, filled with controversy and ideas that push the boundaries of what’s socially acceptable, making it an easy game to turn your nose up at. I would by no means recommend it to just anyone. But this didn’t make for an unenjoyable experience, there are countless characters with a lot to love here and truly something (or someone) for everyone. Are you a sucker for childhood friends falling for each other while learning more about one another’s past? You’ll adore Koujaku. Or does the idea of falling for your rival while slowly teaching him to view the world from a new perspective speak to you? Then Noiz is perfect. I could go on. _Dramatical Murder_ is one of those games where it’s unlikely someone will like every part of it, but they will have a part they love deeply. Not to mention, the game really has some stellar music. I don’t usually look for music in a visual novel, but _Dramatical Murder_ has a track for every scenario. Not to mention, every bad ending gets its own personalized song. I’ll probably be listening to “Feel Your Noize” for years to come, such a banger.

Assuming you're looking for a boys’ love visual novel, this is a great entry to consider. Be prepared to stomach some uncomfortable and taboo content, including sexual assault. If you feel prepared for any of that, this game can be a wonderful experience.
